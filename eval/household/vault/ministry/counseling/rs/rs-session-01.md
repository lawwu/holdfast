# Case Report — R.S. — Session 1

SYNTHETIC case report for testing. R.S. and her family are fictional.

Date: 2026-09-10 · Session: 1 · Length of Session: 75 minutes

## What can you highlight as significant background information?

R.S. is 34, married 9 years to T.S. (36), an electrician. They have two children, a daughter (7) and a son (4). She works mornings as a teacher's aide at an elementary school. She grew up the oldest of four with a critical mother; she says she learned early that "if everyone is happy with me, I'm safe." She professed faith at 15 at a church camp. The family has attended Hillcrest for three years and is in a small group. Her mother-in-law, E.S., lives ten minutes away and is closely involved with the children. Pastor R.'s wife referred her.

## Give a summary of reason(s) the counselee came for counseling—the presentation problem(s).

At the women's retreat in late August, R.S. was unexpectedly asked to pray aloud for the group and had what she describes as a panic attack: racing heart, shaking, unable to breathe, and she left the room. Since then she has avoided small group twice and lies awake replaying conversations. She says she has "always been a worrier" but this is the first time it has stopped her from doing things.

Safety: I asked whether she has had any thoughts of harming herself or that life is not worth living. She said no, clearly, and T.S. confirmed by phone that he has no concerns. Her physician saw her in early September and ruled out a thyroid problem; we agreed she will keep her physician informed of anything new.

## What was gained by performing the homework from last session? What change?

First session; no prior homework. She completed the Personal Data Inventory before coming.

## What was your planned agenda for this session? Was it accomplished? If not, why not?

Data gathering, review of the PDI, a safety check, and giving hope. Accomplished.

## What main problems were discussed in this session?

Panic at the retreat; avoidance of small group; constant worry about what others think, especially E.S. and the women in small group; people-pleasing.

## What Biblical solutions were presented in this session? What Scripture?

Matthew 6:25-34, Jesus' teaching on worry, introduced. 1 Peter 5:7 for hope: cast all your anxieties on him, because he cares for you.

## What specific applications to the counselee's life did you discuss from your Scripture?

That her worry is not a personality trait to be managed but a place where God invites her to trust him. We talked through the retreat moment: what she was afraid would happen if she prayed badly.

## What are you seeing (or not) about the counselee's faith in who God is, about their relationship with Jesus, and/or about the role of the Gospel in their daily life and problem?

She speaks warmly of Jesus and knows the gospel well, but describes God as someone she must not disappoint, much like her mother. She has not connected his acceptance in Christ to her fear of others' opinions.

## What unbiblical habits of thinking and/or behaving (preconditioning issues) are you seeing in the counselee?

Rehearsing conversations at night; avoiding situations where she might be evaluated; over-apologizing; saying yes to every request.

## What ruling thoughts, desires, and/or convictions of the heart are emerging or still present?

Early, but a strong desire for approval and to avoid criticism is emerging.

## What homework was given and how did it specifically apply to problems?

1. Worry journal: each time she notices anxiety, write the situation, what she feared would happen, and who she was thinking about.
2. Read Matthew 6:25-34 every day.
3. Attend small group this week, even if she says nothing.

## If someone asked the counselee right after the session, "What did you learn that you needed to change, with God's help?" what would you want him to say? What challenge did you give?

"My worry tells me what I'm trusting." Challenge: go to small group even though she is afraid.

## How was hope or encouragement given in this session?

1 Peter 5:7 and the fact that she came on her own initiative. Many believers have walked through this same fear.

## How is the overall counseling process progressing? What issues have been sufficiently addressed by you and are changing in the counselee?

Just begun. Safety check done; physician involved.

## What is your goal for the next session? Why? If you are past the 3rd session, does it follow your Tentative Counseling Plan?

Review the worry journal and begin teaching the fear of man from Proverbs 29:25.

## Thinking back on/listening to this session, what could you have done better?

I moved too quickly to Scripture before she had finished describing the retreat; she needed more time to be heard.
