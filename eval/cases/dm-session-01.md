# Case Report — D.M. — Session 1

SYNTHETIC case report for testing. D.M. and his family are fictional.

Date: 2026-08-06 · Session: 1 · Length of Session: 75 minutes

## What can you highlight as significant background information?

D.M. is 38, married 11 years to K.M. (36), who homeschools their three children: a son (9), a daughter (6) and a son (3). He is a project manager at a commercial construction company and was promoted in March, which added longer hours and more pressure. He grew up with a father who yelled often and spanked in anger; his mother was passive and kept the peace. He professed faith at 19 through a college private. The family attends church most Sundays but he is not in a small group. Pastor R. referred him.

## Give a summary of reason(s) the participant came for support—the presentation problem(s).

In late July, during an argument over the credit card bill, D.M. yelled at K.M. in front of the children and then punched a hole in the drywall in the garage. K.M. told him that if he did not get help she would take the children to stay with her parents for a while. He describes himself as having "a temper like my dad" and says he loses it "maybe once or twice a week."

Safety: I asked both separately whether he has ever hit, pushed, restrained or threatened K.M. or the children. Both said no, and K.M. said she feels physically safe but "on edge." There are no weapons concerns. We agreed that any physical aggression toward K.M. or the children means K.M. and the children go to her parents, and I inform the church elders.

## What was gained by performing the homework from last session? What change?

First session; no prior homework. He completed the Personal Data Inventory before coming.

## What was your planned agenda for this session? Was it accomplished? If not, why not?

Data gathering, review of the PDI, a safety assessment, and giving hope. Accomplished, though data gathering ran long.

## What main problems were discussed in this session?

Explosive anger at home, mostly with K.M. and the oldest son; blame-shifting ("she knows money stresses me out"); seeing anger as personality rather than sin.

## What Biblical solutions were presented in this session? What Scripture?

James 4:1-3 introduced briefly: quarrels come from desires that battle within us. 1 Corinthians 10:13 for hope.

## What specific applications to the participant's life did you discuss from your Scripture?

That the credit card argument was not caused by K.M. or the bill but by something he wanted badly enough to fight for.

## What are you seeing (or not) about the participant's faith in who God is, about their relationship with Jesus, and/or about the role of the Gospel in their daily life and problem?

He speaks of God as distant and of Christianity mostly as church attendance. He has not connected the gospel to his anger at all.

## What unbiblical habits of thinking and/or behaving (preconditioning issues) are you seeing in the participant?

Learned pattern from his father; blame-shifting; minimizing ("it's just yelling"); venting as release.

## What ruling thoughts, desires, and/or convictions of the heart are emerging or still present?

Too early to say; possibly control over money and a desire for respect.

## What homework was given and how did it specifically apply to problems?

1. Anger log: for each episode write the situation, what I wanted, what I did, and the result.
2. Read James 4:1-10 every day.
3. Write a paragraph on how his anger has affected K.M. and each child.

## If someone asked the participant right after the session, "What did you learn that you needed to change, with God's help?" what would you want him to say? What challenge did you give?

"My anger is my responsibility, not K.'s." Challenge: stop blaming the bill and the kids.

## How was hope or encouragement given in this session?

1 Corinthians 10:13: this temptation is common to man and God provides a way of escape. He came voluntarily, which is a good sign.

## How is the overall private process progressing? What issues have been sufficiently addressed by you and are changing in the participant?

Just begun. Safety plan in place.

## What is your goal for the next session? Why? If you are past the 3rd session, does it follow your Tentative Private Plan?

Review the anger log and teach James 4:1-3 fully, to move him from "temper" to heart desires.

## Thinking back on/listening to this session, what could you have done better?

I talked too much and interrupted him before he finished describing the July incident.
