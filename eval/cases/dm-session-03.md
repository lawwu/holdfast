# Case Report — D.M. — Session 3

SYNTHETIC case report for testing. D.M. and his family are fictional.

Date: 2026-08-20 · Session: 3 (joint session with K.M.) · Length of Session: 90 minutes

## What can you highlight as significant background information?

K.M. attended. She grew up in a calm home and says she "didn't know what to do" with his anger early in the marriage, so she went quiet.

## Give a summary of reason(s) the participant came for support—the presentation problem(s).

Explosive anger in the marriage and toward the children (see Session 1).

## What was gained by performing the homework from last session? What change?

Log completed: five episodes, down from six. He memorized James 1:19-20 and said it in the truck most evenings. The soft-answer exercise was half done.

## What was your planned agenda for this session? Was it accomplished? If not, why not?

Hear K.M.'s perspective; teach Ephesians 4:25-32 on communication; assign confession. Mostly accomplished, but K.M.'s part ran long.

## What main problems were discussed in this session?

K.M. said she "walks on eggshells" and that the children hide in their rooms when they hear his truck pull in. D.M. minimized: "I've never hit anyone." Both spoke harshly about the other's faults.

## What Biblical solutions were presented in this session? What Scripture?

Ephesians 4:25-32, taught as the four rules of communication: speak truth, deal with anger before the sun goes down, attack the problem not the person, and act rather than react. Matthew 7:3-5 (the log and the speck). James 5:16 and Matthew 5:23-24 on confession.

## What specific applications to the participant's life did you discuss from your Scripture?

"I've never hit anyone" measures himself by the wrong standard. Ephesians 4:31 names bitterness, wrath, anger, clamor and slander, all of which he does. He must deal with his own log before pointing at K.M.'s speck.

## What are you seeing (or not) about the participant's faith in who God is, about their relationship with Jesus, and/or about the role of the Gospel in their daily life and problem?

Defensive at first. When K.M. described the children hiding, he became quiet and teary. Conviction may be starting.

## What unbiblical habits of thinking and/or behaving (preconditioning issues) are you seeing in the participant?

Minimizing; comparing himself to his father ("at least I don't do what he did"); keeping score of K.M.'s faults.

## What ruling thoughts, desires, and/or convictions of the heart are emerging or still present?

Respect and control, as before. Self-righteousness: "I'm not as bad as my dad."

## What homework was given and how did it specifically apply to problems?

1. Confess specifically to K.M. and then to the children, naming the sin without excuse (James 5:16, Matthew 5:23-24).
2. The Four Rules of Communication daily heartwork sheet, every day this week.

## If someone asked the participant right after the session, "What did you learn that you needed to change, with God's help?" what would you want him to say? What challenge did you give?

"My family is afraid of me, and that is my sin, not just a bad habit." Challenge: confess without saying "but."

## How was hope or encouragement given in this session?

K.M. said she wants the marriage to work and has seen him try this past week.

## How is the overall private process progressing? What issues have been sufficiently addressed by you and are changing in the participant?

Fewer episodes. Minimizing is the main obstacle now.

## What is your goal for the next session? Why? If you are past the 3rd session, does it follow your Tentative Private Plan?

Teach put off / put on and give him a concrete plan for the moment anger rises.

Tentative Private Plan: (1) heart desires behind anger, James 4; (2) confession and communication, Ephesians 4:25-32; (3) put off / put on and a pause plan; (4) forgiveness and bitterness; (5) work and identity; (6) loving his wife, Ephesians 5; (7) graduation and accountability.

## Thinking back on/listening to this session, what could you have done better?

I let the joint session drift into K.M.'s list of complaints for about 20 minutes. I should have kept the focus on his responsibility and set boundaries for the joint time.
